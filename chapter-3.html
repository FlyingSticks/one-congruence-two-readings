The seam

Where the two readings fuse — and the arithmetic of the agreement

The two readings of the congruence do not merely resemble each other somewhere; they fuse — drawn lines coincide, carrying — on an exact locus of the : the diagonal ru = rv together with the two zero edges, three concurrent lines through the orthographic corner. Off that locus every diagnostic of disagreement — the whisker, the bow, the copy separation — is a unit multiple of one cubic, Δ = rurv(rv − ru). This chapter states the theorem, then follows the agreement into its arithmetic: it is harmonic, and the whole classical harmonic apparatus — the pencil on the horizon, the mirror circle, the — turns out to be the seam seen from different chairs.

Seam theorem

For a rate pair (ru, rv), the following are equivalent:

  1. the two readings are fused — every drawn line is straight under both, with reciprocal graduations;
  2. the gauge line is invariant under the Reciprocal;
  3. along every ruling, the mirror-circle relation |M||I| = |P|² holds per ray;
  4. Δ = rurv(rv − ru) = 0.

3.2 · The harmonic thread

The two bargains of Chapter 2 are struck with the same coin. In a classical two-point perspective, the appears on the horizon for free: the vanishing points of a rectangle's edges and of its plan diagonals sit in harmonic position, (VL, VR; V45, V135) = −1, for any station point and any orientation — because the diagonals bisect the edge directions, and bisectors form a harmonic pencil with their arms. Every draftsman who has used a 45° measuring point has used this fact without naming it.

The cross-slit rig manufactures the same value, but at a depth. Each slit magnifies its own axis by the perspective factor for its own depth; their ratio — the aspect a frontal square casts — is a Möbius map of depth, and it passes through −1 exactly at the harmonic mean of the slit depths, z* = 2ZvZh/(Zv+Zh) = 187.5 in the rig. That locus is the neutral surface: the depth at which the four canonical points of a ray sit in harmonic position. Perspective produces −1 across a direction; the cross-slit produces it at a depth. Same invariant, different chair.

On the seam the agreement acquires its exact arithmetic. In the cross-section through the picture-plane diagonal, the glide mark M, the cross-slit image I, and the pierce point P of each ray satisfy VM·VI = VP²: M and I are whose radius is the geometric mean of the slit depths. The assembly draws the identity ray by ray.

3.3 · The instrument with a dial

The two projections are one adjustable thing. Split the station point into two slits at Zv = Zp−D/2 and Zh = Zp+D/2. At D = 0 the drawing is a one-point perspective. Open the dial: the vanishing structure splits, the aspect of a frontal square begins to encode depth, and the neutral surface detaches from the eye and slides forward by D²/4Zp. Push wider and the depth cue sharpens while the clean band narrows, exactly as the budget identity of Chapter 6 requires. One knob runs the whole family — pinhole at one end, wide glide at the other — and the office method of the 1984 book is this construction performed at a chosen D. This is where the reader stops reading and starts turning the knob.

3.4 · The correction

Glide's cost is a per-depth anisotropy — and the whole reason the cost is payable is that it is the same everywhere on the sheet. At the working depth the frontal plane maps by one affine stretch, identical at every field position, so one reciprocal scale undoes it everywhere: read the gauge AR(z₀) from a known square, multiply the compressed axis by 1/AR(z₀), and the sheet is square at z₀ to machine precision. This is anamorphic correction in the classical sense the 1984 book indexes, now given its coefficient — not trial-and-error unsquaring but a computed reciprocal read straight off the depth, the neutral surface's harmonic balance transported to the working plane. Two honest boundaries: the squaring is exact only on the reference plane, with residual growing linearly in depth of field; and it addresses the first-order anisotropy, not the second-order bow, which stays subordinate inside any band worth squaring. A ruler's worth of work, where perspective's radial error admits no global repair at all.
