One Congruence, Two Readings · Chapter 4

The group

Six numbers, not one — the involutions, the 3-cycle, and one S₃ per channel colliding on the seam

A cross-ratio is one number attached to four points only after an ordering is chosen. Reorder them and the number runs through six values, λ, 1−λ, 1/λ, 1/(1−λ), λ/(λ−1), (λ−1)/λ, permuted by the S₃. Chapter 2 met two of those maps as the involutions of the two registers, the Complement and the Reciprocal, and found their still points were the six special values. This chapter names all six, shows that every one of them is a quantity the drafting board already computes, and then does the thing the book is for: it puts one copy of the group on each channel of the two-slit rig and asks where the two copies agree. The answer is the seam again, and only at harmonic position on it.

The gauge is the group

Theorem. The six named quantities of the board — the mark, the gauge product, the armature coupling, the glide factor, the directing gauge and its return — are the six elements of S₃ acting on one parameter λ. On normalized depth ζ = z/Zp, the board's foreshortening w(z) = Zp/(Zp−z) is exactly the 3-cycle ω and its inverse z(w) is exactly ω²; ω³ = identity.

Observation. ω's orbit {0, 1, ∞} is {picture plane, station point, plane at infinity}: the 3-cycle rotates the three fundamental planes of the perspective apparatus into one another.

Struck from the ledger: "the board independently discovers S₃." It cannot — τ∘σ = ω identically, so once two involutions act on one parameter the 3-cycles exist by force. Their presence is not evidence; only the meaning of the orbit is.

4.1 · All six, named

The three are drawn as symmetric arcs to a partner, each with its pair of still points. The two cannot be drawn that way — they have no partner and no real still point — so they get directed arrows and their own orbit figure. That difference in drawing is the content. The Reciprocal is the camera factor, the gauge product λ·ρ = 1 of Chapter 3's VM·VI = VP². The Third, λ ↦ λ/(λ−1), is the armature coupling, 1/λ + 1/σ = 1 — and the single element that swaps the two slit feet once the surface is pinned at 0, the pairing switch of Chapter 2. The Complement is the glide factor. And ω = 1/(1−λ) is the gD, the same 1/(1−k) that seats the directing axis on the six core armatures: depth into foreshortening, with ω² the return.

The two figures are doing opposite jobs. The triangle shows what the 3-cycle does — it moves, always, cycling the three planes every perspective construction is built on. The inset shows the only place it stops, and that place is not on the board. Those are one statement: the rotation of picture plane, station and infinity has no real fixed configuration, which is also why the camera conjugate and the armature conjugate — the orange and teal images of the v1 sheet — never meet at any real depth. The involutions gave the six special values as still points; the 3-cycles give the apparatus itself as an orbit.

4.2 · Where the orbit shrinks

The generic orbit has six values. It collapses only where λ has a stabiliser, and , so fewer images always means more symmetry holding λ still, never the reverse. Over the reals there are exactly two ways this happens. The {−1, ½, 2}: three values, each fixed by a different involution. The {0, 1, ∞}: the same count, the other triple. The third collapse, six to two, needs the 3-cycle to stand still, λ² − λ + 1 = 0, and that quadratic has discriminant −3 and real minimum ¾: the stratum is empty on the real line and lives at ±60° on the unit circle. The complete invariant of an orbit is  = 256(λ²−λ+1)³/(λ²(λ−1)²): 1728 on the harmonic orbit, 0 at the equianharmonic poles, ∞ on the degenerate triple.

4.3 · One copy per channel

In double-slit geometry the invariant of four rays is not one number but a pair, ⟦r₁, r₂; r₃, r₄⟧ = (λ, μ) — one cross-ratio per slit, a single cross-ratio valued in ℝ ⊕ ℝ. Reordering the rays reorders both foot-quadruples at once, so S₃ acts , σ·(λ, μ) = (σλ, σμ), and the orbit of a pair has size 6/|Stab(λ) ∩ Stab(μ)|. The intersection is the whole story.

Orbit collapse is a chain phenomenon

For a non-degenerate pair (λ, μ) over ℝ ⊕ ℝ, the diagonal orbit has size 6 unless λ = μ ∈ {−1, 2, ½}, where it has size 3. The classical six-to-three collapse survives in double-slit geometry only on the real diagonal — only for quadruples that are the image of one straight line — and only at harmonic position on that line.

The reason is the one just given: each harmonic value is fixed by a different involution, so two harmonic coordinates share a stabiliser only when they are the same value. The two configurations one would expect to collapse, and which do not, are the negative controls. A split-harmonic quadruple (λ = −1, μ generic) keeps a full six-element orbit. So does a mismatched bi-harmonic one (λ = −1, μ = 2 — harmonic on both slits, by different involutions): six again. Only the harmonic quadruple collapses — four scene points on one line, the fourth at the harmonic conjugate — to exactly three pairs, {(−1,−1), (2,2), (½,½)}. So the stratification carries an orbit count: split-harmonic and mismatched bi-harmonic are six-fold strata that merely contain harmonic values, and membership in the collapsed stratum certifies harmonicity and co-chaining at once. The pair of j-invariants is a diagonal invariant but not a complete one — (λ, λ) and (λ, 1/λ) have identical j-pairs and lie in different orbits — and what the j-pair forgets is exactly the skew defect: the orbit remembers chain membership.

The collapse the double slit cannot have follows at once. λ² − λ + 1 = 0 would have to hold in each component separately, and it holds in neither. Of the Benz trinity the equianharmonic collapse belongs to the elliptic member, ℂ, where the Overflow studies stage it; the split member can be harmonic but never equianharmonic. Six to three is the deepest collapse this geometry admits, and it is reserved for chains.

And the rig points at that stratum. On the ray through a scene point, the four canonical points sit in harmonic position, (F₁, F₂; S, P) = −1, exactly when z = 2ZvZh/(Zv+Zh) = 187.5 — the neutral surface of Chapter 3, recovered blind by root-finding in the check. There the two magnifications have equal magnitude and opposite parity, AR(z*) = −1, so the six reorderings of the canonical quadruple give three readings at z* and six everywhere else. One invariant, three appearances: the orbit collapse of the reordering group, the parity balance of the magnifications, and the fusion depth of the doubled camera in Chapter 6 are a single harmonic fact read in three charts.

4.4 · Why these values and no others

The six special values, the two real collapses and the one complex one are not a coincidence of the board. Promote the six maps from verbs to nouns and one new verb appears, composition; climb to the symmetries of that and the tower stops growing — S₃ is its own automorphism group, the ceiling. And the whole story has one root at the top: the modular group is ℤ/2 ∗ ℤ/3, carrying rotations of order 2 and 3 and no others, whose fixed points i and ρ are the only special points there are, and the fixed-point equation of the order-3 one is τ² − τ + 1 = 0 — the equianharmonic polynomial itself. The companions climb that tower by hand; the book needs only the floor it stands on, where the two channels' copies of the group collide on the seam.
