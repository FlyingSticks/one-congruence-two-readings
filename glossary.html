Glossary

Every term the chapters define on tap, collected once — 57 entries, each linked to the chapters that use it

The definitions here are the ones the chapters show when a dotted term is tapped, reconciled where two chapters had said the same thing in different words. Where one word carries two meanings in the book — gauge as the depth scale of Chapter 2 and as the physicists' free description of Chapter 8; reciprocal as the involution ρ and as the relation between the two graduations — both are entered and cross-named. Each entry links to itself, so a term can be cited as glossary.html#neutral. The rig throughout: picture plane at z = 0, Zp = 200, D = 100, Zv = 150, Zh = 250, z* = 187.5. The archive's earlier glossary, from the glide side alone, remains at Glide_projection/the-glossary-v1.

3-cycleCh. 4
ω = 1/(1−λ) and ω² = (λ−1)/λ, with ω³ = identity. No partner, no real still point; fixed only at e±iπ/3. τ∘σ = ω, so two involutions on one parameter force the 3-cycles to exist.
Anamorphic dissociationCh. 8
The double slit's factorization of concurrence by coordinate: x-meetings deported to one slit, y-meetings to the other, at two different depths. The pinhole spends its whole ∞² ray budget at one point; the double slit spends ∞¹ along each slit, and the mismatch between the two partial organizations is the depth signal.
AnchorCh. 5
The quadric whose pole–polar map is the chosen duality. The rig's canonical anchor is the sphere x² + y² + z² = Z₁Z₂: centre at the principal point, radius the geometric mean of the slit depths, slits mutually polar. Anharmonicity of the transport selects it.
Anharmonic groupCh. 4
The six Möbius maps that permute a cross-ratio under reordering of its four points — λ, 1−λ, 1/λ, 1/(1−λ), λ/(λ−1), (λ−1)/λ — isomorphic to S₃. Reordering acts through S₄; the Klein four-group acts trivially.
ArmatureCh. 1, 7
The drawn incidence skeleton — lines, concurrences, axes, picture plane — the shadow of the congruence on the sheet. Shared by both readings; only the graduation along it differs. The six core armatures of the 1984 plate (p. 93) are the six patterns of recession one gauge can produce, indexed by the rate pair.
Armature lawCh. 2
The shape of the image machinery: per-channel linear contraction toward a seat at a rate — straight depth lines, a rate pair, the vanishing structure. Summarized by (ru, rv).
BudgetCh. 6
The quantitative form of the bargain: a projection has a fixed quantity of fidelity, allocated between depth and breadth by the dial D. The criterion compares Δ·D (the glide's residual anisotropy, linear in depth extent and separation) against B² (perspective's marginal splay, quadratic in breadth).
CalibratedCh. 8
Vision's word for the metric rung: a camera is calibrated once its intrinsics are known, which is exactly what upgrades a reconstruction from projective or affine to metric. Here, nominating the screen supplies the three fiducials that turn the depth gauge into a coordinate. Of the three names for the rung, the only one that names the act of pinning structure to numbers.
CalibrationCh. 2
A depth plane at which readings are pinned to agree; selects the gauge's representative. Glide practice's staging, generalized.
ChainCh. 8
The image of a line skew to both slits: the set of rays meeting it, which on the screen is the graph of a Möbius map — a hyperbola with axis-parallel asymptotes, the split-signature sibling of the circle. Closed, as a bidegree-(1,1) curve in ℙ¹ × ℙ¹. Lines and circles fuse into it.
Co-chainedCh. 4
Four scene points lying on one straight line, so that both slits see the same cross-ratio, λ = μ. The only quadruples on which the six-to-three collapse survives in double-slit geometry — and only at harmonic position.
Complement τCh. 2
x ↦ 1 − x. Reach's involution, the fold about the midpoint of the unit; fixes ½ and ∞.
Cross-ratio along the rayCh. 6
(F₁, F₂; S, P): the two slit feet, the screen point and the scene point on one line. Theorem 7: it equals the aspect ratio a round target at P casts. Depth is therefore a projective coordinate, invertible by a Möbius map.
Cross-slitCh. 1, 3
The optical construction: the congruence read as rays through two slits at two depths, graduated by foreshortening (the Turn gauge). Depth lines bow. Drawn purple.
Degenerate orbitCh. 4
{0, 1, ∞}: two of the four points coincide. Same orbit size as harmonic, the other triple; on the board these are the picture plane, station and plane at infinity. j = ∞.
Depth gaugeCh. 2
The scale laid along the depth lines: Reach (equal steps, ∞ unmarked) or Turn (Möbius, ∞ at a finite mark) — the two graduations of Theorem 0. Free in the glide, welded in the cross-slit.
Depth gauge (Theorem 5.3)Ch. 6
The map that fixes both slits pointwise and slides every point along its own ray. The cross-slit image is frozen by it: depth is exactly the coordinate the image group cannot see, and calibration (nominating the screen and the metric) is what turns that invisible freedom into a measured number.
Diagonal actionCh. 4
One reordering of the four rays reorders both slit foot-quadruples at once: σ·(λ, μ) = (σλ, σμ). The orbit of the pair has size 6/|Stab(λ) ∩ Stab(μ)|.
Directing gauge gDCh. 4
The board's 1/(1−k), which seats the directing axis on the six core armatures. It is the 3-cycle ω: on normalized depth it carries picture plane → station → plane at infinity → back.
DualityCh. 5
A correlation of projective space: points ↔ planes, lines ↔ lines, joins ↔ meets, incidence preserved. Not one map but a whole family; a specific one, δ, must be chosen by an anchor.
EquianharmonicCh. 4
λ² − λ + 1 = 0, λ = e±iπ/3: the orbit shrinks to two, fixed by the 3-cycle. Empty over the reals (discriminant −3, real minimum ¾). j = 0.
Fixed elementCh. 5
The one anharmonic map by which δ-conjugation of the glide reading differs from the cross-slit reading: ρ, the Reciprocal — arriving with the apparatus channel swap. Since ρτ = ω it converts the glide gauge τ into the camera gauge ω.
Gauge (physicists’ sense)Ch. 8
A freedom of description the nominated structure has not yet pinned — an unbroken symmetry the group still moves. Depth is a free gauge at the projective rung (Theorem 5.3); the fiducials fix it at the metric rung; absolute scale waits for the Euclidean one. Distinct from the depth gauge of Chapter 2, though the document's Theorem 5.3 is where the two senses meet.
GlideCh. 1, 3
The 1984 drawing construction: the congruence read as edges under parallel light, depth lines straight, graduated in equal steps (the Reach gauge). Drawn teal.
Harmonic cross-ratioCh. 1, 3
Four collinear points with cross-ratio −1. Perspective manufactures it across the horizon; the cross-slit manufactures it at the neutral surface; on sheet 35·G the glide's equal depth marks fall in harmonic progression along the cross-slit's ray. Its orbit under reordering is {−1, ½, 2}.
Harmonic orbitCh. 4
{−1, ½, 2}: cross-ratio −1 and its two reorderings. Each value is fixed by a different involution (−1 by the Reciprocal, ½ by the Complement, 2 by the Third). j = 1728.
HoropterCh. 6
In binocular vision, the surface of zero disparity through the fixation point. In the doubled rig it is the neutral surface z* = 187.5, where D = 1 and the channels fuse — fixed by the rig rather than by vergence.
InvariantCh. 2
What holds at every setting — Turn-side truth. The cross-ratio is the invariant every reading shares: three-pin coordinates on one line all report the same cross-ratio of four depths.
InvolutionCh. 4
A map that is its own inverse. S₃ has three: the Reciprocal 1/λ, the Complement 1−λ, and the Third λ/(λ−1). Each fixes one harmonic value and one degenerate one.
j-invariantCh. 4
j(λ) = 256(λ²−λ+1)³/(λ²(λ−1)²), the complete invariant of a single orbit: 1728 harmonic, 0 equianharmonic, ∞ degenerate. For a pair (λ, μ) the j-pair is invariant but not complete.
Jensen biasCh. 6
On the working band the decode z(AR) is convex, so symmetric noise in AR pushes the expected depth outward — E[ẑ] − z > 0. A property of the geometry, not the solver.
JointCh. 7
A cell boundary where two armatures meet. Rule 1 (no gaps or overlaps) makes the shear on the far side of a joint a forced consequence of the seat and the joint's position, not a choice.
Line congruenceCh. 1
A two-parameter family of lines: through a general point of space passes one line of the family, and in a general plane lies one. The two-slit congruence is the set of transversals of two skew slit lines. The only object in the book.
Mirror circleCh. 3
The circle, radius the geometric mean of the slit depths, in which glide mark and cross-slit image are inverse points on the seam: VM·VI = VP².
Neutral surfaceCh. 3
The depth z* = 2ZvZh/(Zv+Zh) — the harmonic mean of the slit depths — where the two slits' magnifications exactly cancel and the aspect ratio passes through −1. Drawn gold throughout.
Orbit–stabiliserCh. 4
|orbit| × |stabiliser| = 6 for every λ. Generic 6 × 1; harmonic 3 × 2; degenerate 3 × 2; equianharmonic 2 × 3 — the four ways six factors.
Page readingCh. 5
The projective parameter of a pencil of planes through a slit, read off on the picture plane as a range of trace lines. The cross-slit coordinate X₀/(1 − λru) is exactly this parameter — a theorem, not a numerical question.
PinCh. 2
A distinguished point that fixes one of the three Möbius freedoms of a coordinate on a line. Three pins make a ruler; the cross-slit's are placed by optics, the glide's are chosen.
PinholeCh. 1
The degenerate congruence in which both slits sit at one depth: every line passes through one centre, and the two readings coincide as maps. Classical perspective.
Plane at infinityCh. 2
The fourth distinguished point of a line. A pin for both glide gauges (Reach sends it to ∞, Turn to 1); a probe for the cross-slit, whose image 1 − Z₂/Z₁ moves with the slits.
PolarityCh. 5
A duality that is its own inverse, given by a symmetric matrix — pole and polar with respect to a conic or quadric. A canonical duality is exactly a marked polarity; the straightedge draws none, the compass draws one.
PushbroomCh. 1
The congruence with one slit at a finite depth and the other at infinity — a corner of the rate square, (1,0) or (0,1). Fused under both readings.
Rate squareCh. 1, 3, 7
The space of readings: a point (ru, rv) gives the linear contraction rate of each channel. Optical constructions sit on the corners and edges; glide constructions fill the interior.
ReachCh. 2
The additive register: equal steps mean equal amounts; the slide preserves it; ∞ is pinned at ∞; the only involution is the Complement. The count, the covector, the stack of level planes.
ReadingCh. 1, 2
A congruence given a role — a rule for what the lines are (edges or rays) together with a graduation laid along them; exactly, a law together with a class of gauges (Chapter 2). Glide and cross-slit are readings of one congruence, not rival skeletons.
Reciprocal graduationsCh. 3
The two readings mark the same line with graduations that are inverses of each other — their product is one. Where the marks coincide, the readings fuse.
Reciprocal ρCh. 2
x ↦ 1/x. Turn's involution, inversion in the mirror circle of radius 1; fixes ±1, exchanges 0 and ∞. With τ it generates the whole anharmonic group.
Self-dualCh. 5
A configuration carried to itself by the anchoring duality. The class of cross-slit congruences is self-dual (meeting is self-dual); on the seam a single configuration is its own mirror image — fused means self-dual.
Shape disparityCh. 6
In the doubled rig the two channels measure reciprocal aspect ratios; their ratio D(z) = ARA² is the disparity signal, and it lives in shape rather than position. Confounds that multiply both channels alike cancel in it exactly.
SlitCh. 1
A line in a depth plane that every ray of the congruence must meet. The rig has a vertical slit at Zv = 150 (crimson) and a horizontal slit at Zh = 250 (purple); each magnifies its own axis by the perspective factor for its own depth.
SpineCh. 5
The line every plane of a book passes through — under duality, a slit. Cross-slit (u, v) is the two page readings of the two books whose spines are the slits.
StackCh. 5
The glide's graduated family of parallel level planes — Reach's covector picture. Its dual in P³ is a book of planes.
StationCh. 2
The point the Turn graduation sends to ∞ — the third pin, and the only one that is not a mark on the drawing. In perspective, the eye. Reach has no station.
TorsorCh. 5
A set acted on freely and transitively by a group, with no distinguished element. The correlations of Pⁿ are a torsor under the projective group: any duality composed with any projectivity is another duality, so none is canonical without an anchor.
TransportCh. 5
The map depth → depth induced by δ, read through the depth axis: z† = Z₁Z₂/z, an involution fixed at ±√(Z₁Z₂). In geometric-mean calibration it is the Reciprocal 1/ν.
TurnCh. 2
The multiplicative register: equal steps mean equal ratios; the scale preserves it; ∞ is a marked point, so a station is sent to ∞ in its place; depth steps become the harmonic progression k/(k+1). Foreshortening, perspective, the cross-slit with both feet finite.
Type A transferCh. 7
Marks carried along a fibration — every depth station receives a true copy, and the fraction of the way along an edge is preserved exactly at every depth. The collector is the seat, where every part mark's depth line concurs. Contrast Type B sighting, marks collected through a point.
