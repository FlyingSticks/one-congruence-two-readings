All six, named — three involutions, two 3-cycles, and the three planes they rotate

Every one of the six anharmonic maps is a quantity the board already computes. The three involutions are drawn as symmetric arcs to a partner, each with its pair of still points. The two 3-cycles can’t be drawn that way — they have no partner and no real still point — so they get directed arrows instead, and their own orbit figure. That difference is the content: ω = 1/(1−λ) is your directing gauge gᴅ, and it rotates the three fundamental planes of the perspective apparatus into one another — picture plane → station point → plane at infinity → back.
