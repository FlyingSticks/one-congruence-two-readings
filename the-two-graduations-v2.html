0 · The theorem the title names

Theorem 0. A scale on a straight line cannot both mark equal steps and assign infinity a finite mark. A scale with equal steps is affine, and an affine map of the line sends ∞ to ∞.

One line, and every depth practice must live on one side of it. The Reach gauge keeps equal steps and leaves infinity unmarked; the Turn gauge is a Möbius re-scaling that marks infinity at a finite point and pays for it with harmonic crowding. These are the two graduations. Everything below is the anatomy of that choice.

1 · The substrate

Strip both practices to what they share and one incidence skeleton remains: straight lines in the image, their concurrences, the vanishing axes, the picture plane. Call it the armature. Neither practice owns it. Glide draws it with the tinsmith's projectors; cross-slit draws it with light through two slits; on the drafting board they trace the same skeleton.

2 · Law and gauge

Two different things were hiding under one word in v1, and the theory sharpens when they are split. The armature law is the shape of the image machinery: each channel of the picture contracts toward its seat at its own rate — straight depth lines, a rate pair, the vanishing structure. The depth gauge is the scale laid along those lines — which of the two graduations, and pinned where. A reading is a law together with a class of gauges; a calibration selects the representative. So there is no single object called the glide: glide names the law, and the gauge remains to be chosen.

3 · The two readings

Glide construction is the linear armature law with the gauge left free — and the practice uses that freedom. Sheet 39 runs all six core armatures on a gauge that is Möbius in depth, with affine rates laid on top; the measuring board runs the same law on an equal-step gauge and then, at the flip of a chip, on the harmonic one. Straightness survives every re-gauging because the law is affine in the gauge, whatever the gauge is in depth. Cross-slit projection is the same skeleton with no freedom left: the optics weld law and gauge together. Rays through two slits fix both the lines' bowing and the crowding along them; no slider can repeal the bow, and no chip can re-graduate it. That is the precise content of "one is physics, the other is a tool": the tool retains a degree of freedom the optics foreclose.

4 · The interface

The readings meet by calibration: pin them to agree at chosen depth planes. This is not an invention of the board — it is glide practice's own staging, generalized. Sheet 39's gauge plane images identically under all six armatures: "the staging rectangle is the common ground every comparison stands on." The measuring board extends staging across the two practices: level 0 (the front face, true size, in the picture plane) and level 16 (the deepest constructed plane) are shared pins. Between the pins the gauges disagree in character — equal spacing against harmonic crowding. Beyond them the vanishing hierarchy opens, and it is strict: 16 < h pair < v pair. Under the harmonic gauge the hierarchy sharpens into theorem: the depth plane at infinity maps to the flat segment h2 · A · h1 on one vanishing axis — the limit of measured depth — while the v pair lies beyond even that, unreachable at any depth, directional only.

5 · The two balances

The split rectangle distinguishes two kinds of truth. Its multiplicative balance holds for every position of the point — Turn-side truth, an invariant. Its additive balance closes only on the diagonal — Reach-side truth, a calibration. Both practices contain both kinds: glide has its ratio and parallelism invariants, cross-slit has its choices of screen and slits. But their characteristic truths sort by side — the cross-slit's signature laws are invariants (the cross-ratio readout that never moves, the bowing no slider can repeal), the glide's signature truths are measurements — and the interface between readings is purely Reach-side: shared levels are diagonal closures, Euclid I.43 moments, true exactly where the construction puts them. The cross-ratio is the balance that never moved.

6 · The classification

Where can the image of infinity land? The six core armatures are the answer, read off the rate pair per channel: (1, 1) a point catches infinity; (λ, λ) infinity arrives early as an area; (1−k, 1) the vanishing axis with its directing axis; (0, 1) the herringbone — vanishing one way, parallel the other; (0, λ) the keystone; (0, 0) the box, where infinity is never marked at all. The two gut feelings sit at the corners of this table. Reducible to a point: the (1, 1) corner, reached optically by fusing the slits into a pinhole. Broad and shallow: the r → 0 corners, where recession goes parallel, depth stays a measured slab, and composition proceeds laterally by staging — one slab-like expanse of endlessly arranged armatures.

Conjecture, unchecked: since the focal locus of the cross-slit congruence is a pair of lines and lines are self-dual in space, the two-slit rig may sit at the self-dual center of this family, with the point and slab readings as its dual ends. Flagged, not asserted.

7 · The means, exactly

Three calibrations were tried on the way to the board, and they are tied together more tightly than a list of names suggests. Write Xg and Xcs for the two marks of one true measure x. The transpose calibration is the geometric mean: Xg · Xcs = x², so inversion through x exchanges the two marks. The mirrored calibration then splits into a conjugate pair under that very inversion:

x = AM( Xg , x²/Xcs )  and  x = HM( Xcs , x²/Xg )

— the true measure is simultaneously the arithmetic mean of the glide mark with the inverted camera mark, and the harmonic mean of the camera mark with the inverted glide mark. The geometric calibration is the exchanger between the other two. The measured board completes the family: pin two levels and the gauge between them ranges over the Möbius maps, the equal-step and harmonic scales being the two distinguished members.

9 · Provenance

Classical and on record: the crossed-slit image as a (1, 1) linear line congruence, with the pinhole as its point degeneration (Zomet, Feldman, Peleg & Shashua, the crossed-slits camera, c. 2003); Klein's Erlangen program for geometry as invariants; Euclid I.43 for the additive balance; the descriptive-geometry and sheet-metal tradition for developments, transition pieces, and staging. Internal to this practice and prior to this spine: the glide system and its armatures (the author's 1984 study and the Glide_projection sheets, where the staging rectangle and the rate-pair taxonomy already operate). Belonging to the spine itself: the law/gauge split, the calibration of the two practices against each other on shared levels, and the vanishing hierarchy that falls out.
